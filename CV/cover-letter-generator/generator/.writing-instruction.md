# Cover Letter Writing Instructions

Use these instructions as the voice and quality guide for Minn Bo Bo's cover letters.

## Voice

- Write in first person with a direct, sincere tone.
- Sound like a capable software engineer explaining real motivation, not like a generic AI template.
- Keep the English clear and professional. Use natural phrasing, simple sentence structure, and confident wording.
- Use technical terms when they are relevant, but do not overcomplicate the sentence.
- Prefer concrete alignment: company work, job requirements, projects, tools, research interests, and career direction.
- The letter should feel thoughtful and personal, with a steady sense of ambition.

## Style From Samples

- The writing often starts from background, then connects that background to a specific opportunity.
- It uses phrases such as "I am interested in", "my experience", "I have hands-on experience", and "I am confident".
- It explains why an organization or program is attractive, especially when there is a clear technical or research connection.
- It is comfortable mentioning AI, deep learning, full-stack development, cloud systems, web applications, networking, security, and practical implementation.
- It should keep the earnestness of the samples but be more concise for job applications.

## Cover Letter Structure

Use 4 paragraphs.

1. Opening:
   - State the role and company.
   - Mention why the role is interesting.
   - Connect the opportunity to full-stack engineering, backend systems, cloud, DevOps, AI, or the company's domain.

2. Experience Match:
   - Summarize the strongest matching professional experience.
   - Include specific technologies from the job description when they match honestly.
   - Focus on delivery, maintainability, testing, production systems, and collaboration.

3. Motivation and Fit:
   - Explain why the company, product, industry, or engineering challenge matters.
   - Add one specific detail from the job description or company notes.
   - Keep this paragraph personal, not exaggerated.

4. Closing:
   - Reaffirm interest.
   - Say that the candidate would welcome the opportunity to discuss how his experience can contribute.
   - End politely and confidently.

## Content Rules

- Target 250 to 380 words unless the user asks otherwise.
- Avoid overused cover-letter phrases such as "I am writing to express my interest" unless no better opening is available.
- Avoid grand claims such as "perfect fit", "world-class", "revolutionary", or "unparalleled".
- Do not invent experience, employers, credentials, metrics, or publications.
- Do not copy long sentences from the job description.
- Use "software engineer", "full-stack", "backend", "cloud", "DevOps", "AI", "Node.js", "TypeScript", "React", "PostgreSQL", "AWS", "Docker", "CI/CD", "REST APIs", and "testing" only when relevant.
- Do not turn the letter into a technical keyword list. Mention only the strongest 3 to 5 matching tools or domains, then explain the value in plain English.
- If a job requirement does not match directly, frame nearby experience honestly.
- Use contractions sparingly. They can make the voice human, but the final letter should still be formal.
- Do not sound apologetic about gaps or partial matches.

## Output Quality Checklist

- The opening names the role and company.
- Every paragraph has a purpose.
- The letter contains at least two details specific to the job or company.
- The tone is confident, warm, and technically grounded.
- The letter is concise enough to fit on one page.
- The result sounds like Minn Bo Bo, not a generic applicant.
