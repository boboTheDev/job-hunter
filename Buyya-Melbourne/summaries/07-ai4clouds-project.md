# AI for Clouds project page

Source: [CLOUDS Lab AI for Clouds](https://clouds.cis.unimelb.edu.au/ai4clouds/)  
Checked: 7 October 2026

## Summary

The project frames edge/cloud infrastructures as large, interconnected, heterogeneous systems supporting workloads with demanding SLAs. It argues that manual parameter tuning is infeasible and that data-driven AI can capture nonlinear relationships and configure systems more efficiently.

The page lists work on workflow anomaly detection, energy- and thermal-aware VM consolidation, AI-centric resource management, stochastic edge-cloud scheduling, GPU frequency scaling, anomaly-aware deep-RL scaling, thermal prediction, RL-based serverless cold-start reduction, and data-centre workload/energy/temperature characterization. It also points to the UniMelb Cloud Dataset and associated code.

## Critical points

- The page demonstrates exact supervisory fit for AI, resource management, energy, thermal behavior, serverless, and edge-cloud scheduling.
- It also raises the novelty bar: a proposal that simply applies DRL to scheduling, predicts temperature, or reduces cold starts would overlap existing lab work.
- A credible extension should integrate currently separated objectives, enforce safety/SLA constraints, evaluate carbon rather than energy alone, and contribute a deployable and reusable system.
- The public page's team list and some publication statuses may lag current personnel or publication metadata; confirm live supervision arrangements directly.

## Proposal implication

Position the work as an extension of the lab's trajectory: build on its dataset, energy/thermal models, serverless work, and contemporary ReinFog framework, while contributing sustainability-aware constrained orchestration and rigorous deployment validation.
