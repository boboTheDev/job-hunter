# Doctor of Philosophy - Engineering and IT overview

Source: [University of Melbourne course page](https://study.unimelb.edu.au/find/courses/graduate/doctor-of-philosophy-engineering-and-it/)  
Checked: 7 October 2026

## Summary

The degree is listed as four years full-time or eight years part-time, on campus at Parkville, with flexible intake. The page states an expected candidature of three years full-time. Computing and information systems is one of the named research areas, and applicants may pursue a listed project or propose their own.

The course expects an original and significant contribution to knowledge and emphasizes academic leadership, independence, creativity, and innovation. Supervision and advisory committees support the work, and the program sits within a broad engineering and IT research environment.

## Critical points

- “Four years full-time” is the formal duration ceiling, while “expected candidature is three years” should shape the work plan. The proposed core contributions therefore fit within 36 months, leaving the remaining allowance as contingency rather than planned scope.
- Intake is flexible, but scholarship assessment uses fixed rounds. A flexible course start does not mean scholarship deadlines are flexible.
- The page is broad and does not prove that any particular supervisor has capacity. Prof. Buyya's in-principle support remains a gating item.
- The proposal must make a computing/information-systems contribution, not merely report environmental benefits.

## Proposal implications

Frame the research around new resource-management algorithms, a reusable software framework, and repeatable systems experiments. Sustainability is the problem context and objective; the doctoral contribution is in distributed systems and adaptive resource management.
