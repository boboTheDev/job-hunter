## Summary and extracted requirements

The note describes the CLOUDS Laboratory's research agenda in parallel and distributed computing, with an explicit preference for work that combines theory (“inventions”) and deployable systems (“innovations”). Its topic list covers cloud, edge/fog, IoT, market-oriented and utility computing, high-performance distributed computing, quantum computing, distributed storage, data-intensive science, service-oriented computing, and eScience/eBusiness applications.

The requested application package includes certified academic records, recommendation letters, copies of publications, a detailed CV that explains software projects and publications, and a tentative research proposal. The note says the proposal is an initial idea that may change and encourages discussion with a prospective supervisor before formal submission. It asks applicants to contact Prof. Rajkumar Buyya for PhD/MPhil supervision.

## Critical points

- **Strong signal:** the proposal should contain both an algorithmic contribution and a working software/system evaluation. A purely conceptual sustainability essay would be weak.
- **Strong signal:** the application must demonstrate self-direction, research ability, and hands-on implementation.
- **Historical content:** the scholarship labels IPRS/MIRS/MIFRS, the stated August/October deadlines, university size figures, and some URLs belong to an older admissions regime. They must not override the current FEIT course page.
- **Proposal-length conflict:** this note says “not exceeding 4 pages,” while Prof. Buyya's direct instruction asks for a “1-2 proposal.” The safest first-contact attachment is a polished two-page concept note; retain the longer plan as the source document for later formal requirements.
- **Qualification wording is only indicative:** the note's simple PhD-versus-Masters rule is not a substitute for formal equivalency assessment.

## Application implications

Lead with one concrete systems problem, one novel method, an open-source artefact, and measurable experiments. Show how existing AWS, Docker, CI/CD, Python/TensorFlow, backend, and research experience reduce execution risk. Seek explicit in-principle supervision support before relying on scholarship submission.
